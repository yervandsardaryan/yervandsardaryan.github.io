Stolen Card Blocking

From a customer’s theft report to a confirmed protective action: an end-to-end use case covering business decisions, alternative paths, exception handling, process modeling and testable outcomes.

01 / Business context

Protect the customer. Confirm the outcome.

When a payment card is stolen, the customer expects the bank to protect it promptly, confirm what action has occurred, and help with any resulting unauthorized activity. The bank must coordinate a time-sensitive protective process while preserving traceability, respecting authorization controls and separating card protection from later fraud investigation.

Primary actorCustomer / Customer Service Agent
TriggerCustomer reports a stolen card
Business objectivePrevent further unauthorized card usage
Supporting partiesCard Management, Fraud Operations
PriorityEmergency / time-sensitive
ScopeReport → block → confirm → follow up
02 / Readiness

Preconditions

  1. An existing card relationship is present, and the affected card can be identified or identified through an approved escalation path.
  2. The report arrives through an authorized customer-facing channel, such as a branch, call center or digital service.
  3. Approved protection and authorization procedures are available, including a safe approach to emergency requests where ordinary verification cannot be completed.
  4. Card blocking is operational, or an approved contingency process is available.

Business nuance: Emergency protection must not be unnecessarily delayed by routine procedures. Exactly when verification or protective restrictions may occur is a bank-policy decision, not assumed in this illustrative case.

03 / End-to-end process

Stolen-card blocking flow

A BPMN-style model using three actor lanes, tasks, exclusive decision gateways and start/end events. Scroll the diagram horizontally on smaller screens.

This is an illustrative BPMN-style visual, not an executable BPMN 2.0 XML diagram. An implementation-ready model would also define formal message flows, ownership and boundary/error events.

Main success path

  1. Report: The customer reports theft through an authorized service channel.
  2. Identify: Bank staff identify the affected card and apply the relevant identity and authorization policy.
  3. Check: The current card status is evaluated to avoid an unnecessary duplicate action.
  4. Register: The bank records the theft incident and known circumstances.
  5. Protect: If the card is eligible, an immediate block is initiated.
  6. Confirm: The bank verifies the actual blocking outcome, rather than assuming success.
  7. Communicate: The customer receives a confirmed status and clear next steps.
  8. Follow up: Potential fraud/dispute review and replacement are handled as separate decisions.
  9. Trace: Actions, outcomes and outstanding follow-ups remain documented.
04 / Decision coverage

Alternative & exception scenarios

Expand any scenario to see the expected business response. The set includes blocking-state, verification, operational and downstream exceptions.

A1Card already blocked+

Confirm the existing restriction, log the theft report, and continue any fraud or replacement follow-up. Do not imply a new block was executed.

A2Customer cannot complete standard verification+

Follow a defined emergency-protection process; restrict disclosure of account information and escalate any uncertainty.

A3Card cannot be identified+

Gather additional evidence or escalate urgently. Do not block an unrelated card.

A4More than one card was stolen+

Identify each card, request protection for each, and document its own outcome. Failed or uncertain results remain open.

A5Customer suspects unauthorized transactions+

Continue protective blocking and separately initiate fraud review or applicable transaction-dispute handling.

A6Blocking service is unavailable+

Use approved contingency and escalation procedures; do not confirm a successful block until independently verified.

A7Request times out or result is uncertain+

Check authoritative card status before retrying. Record the unknown outcome and escalate when confirmation cannot be obtained.

A8Customer requests a replacement card+

After the stolen card is protected, assess eligibility and initiate a separate replacement process.

A9Customer declines replacement+

Record the choice and keep the stolen-card restriction in force.

A10Customer later finds the card+

Do not automatically reactivate a card reported stolen; apply the organization’s recovery or replacement policy.

A11Card is expired or closed+

Confirm the existing status and document the theft report; assess whether additional security follow-up is required.

A12Customer withdraws the report+

Treat withdrawal as new information, not automatic authorization to reverse a protective block. Follow the security policy.

05 / Completion states

Postconditions

Successful blockCard protection is confirmed, the theft report is registered and the customer has been informed.
Already restrictedExisting status is verified; theft information and necessary follow-up are recorded.
Failure or uncertaintyBlocking is not falsely reported as successful; urgent verification or escalation stays open.
Partial completionThe card may be protected while fraud investigation, dispute handling or replacement remains pending.

A completed protective block does not automatically mean the full customer case is closed.

07 / Business constraints

Business Rules

BR-01Emergency protection is time-sensitive, and the applicable bank policy determines its verification requirements.
BR-02Only a confirmed blocking result may be communicated as completed.
BR-03An existing block does not eliminate the need to document a new theft report.
BR-04Card blocking and unauthorized-transaction investigation are distinct processes.
BR-05Replacement is decided separately from card protection.
BR-06Every affected card needs an individual, traceable status.
BR-07A card marked stolen must not be automatically reactivated.
About this example

Business analysis deliverable

This case study is a fictional, confidentiality-safe interpretation of a stolen-card reporting process. It demonstrates business workflow analysis, process modeling, exceptions, business rules and acceptance criteria; it does not publish proprietary bank procedures, real customer information or actual card-system contracts.
